Mexico's geographical diversity encompasses 1,972,550 square kilometres spanning from mountain ranges to coastal plains and volcanic regions [1]. The Sierra Madre Occidental extends from the northern border southeast through various regions, featuring steep peaks and deep canyons including Copper Canyon, North America's deepest canyon [1]. The country's highest point is the Pico de Orizaba Volcano at 5,636 metres, part of a series of ancient volcanoes stretching from the Pacific Ocean to the Gulf of Mexico [1]. The Central Mexican Plateau covers much of northern and central Mexico at an average elevation of 1,825 metres above sea level, largely covered by deserts and xeric shrublands [1].

Mexico has positioned itself among the top 10 most favoured tourist destinations globally according to official figures from the Secretariat of Tourism, reflecting growth in foreign direct investment, visitor spending, and international traveler income [5]. However, the country experienced a decline in tourist currency attraction in 2023, dropping five places in the global ranking for foreign currency collection to 15th position despite collecting a historic sum of $30,809.5 million [5]. New aviation infrastructure developments include a direct air route linking Italy and Cancun operated by Italian airline Neos, commencing December 22nd with weekly Sunday flights [5].

The country's tourism infrastructure centres on both coastal destinations and cultural sites. The Yucatan Peninsula features limestone composition and tropical jungles, housing archaeological sites including Chichen Itza, Tulum and Coba [5]. The Pueblos Mágicos programme designates towns that preserve historical, cultural and natural heritage through tangible and intangible expressions, representing tourist alternatives for national and foreign visitors [5]. These locations maintain symbols, legends and historical significance that demonstrate national identity [5].

Security conditions vary significantly across Mexico's states, with some regions designated as high-risk areas where emergency services remain limited or unavailable in remote locations [2]. The state of Colima faces particular security challenges due to violence from various criminal organisations, with most homicides constituting targeted assassinations and shooting incidents between groups occasionally affecting bystanders [2]. Transportation restrictions apply in certain areas, including prohibitions on inter-city travel after dark and requirements to use regulated taxi services rather than street-hailed vehicles [2].

Nigeria's swimming community celebrated the conclusion of the seventh Dolphin Swimming League season, with Naetochukwu Oguegbu of Meadow Hall School claiming the Best Male Swimmer award after securing 15 gold medals [7]. A coalition of scholars and governance experts convened virtually for the 2nd International Conference on Followership Studies, warning of potential national systemic collapse due to accountability failures [6]. The conference, organised by PANAFSTRAG in collaboration with Empowered Newswire and anchored by veteran journalist Laolu Akande, focused on citizen responsibility and democratic engagement ahead of the 2027 elections [6]. Contributors including Dr Charles Dickson and Prof. Nimi Wariboko argued that governance outcomes are heavily influenced by the quality and consciousness of citizens, with silence and indifference often sustaining governance failure [6]. The event was dedicated to the memory of Mallam Aminu Kano, described as a defining symbol of citizen-centred political engagement in Nigeria's democratic evolution [6].

The Centre for Public Accountability has identified the Tertiary Education Trust Fund as vital to Nigeria's higher education system, warning that many public institutions would face severe infrastructural decline without its interventions [8]. Between 2011 and 2024, TETFund disbursed more than ₦1.8 trillion to public tertiary institutions, with universities receiving over ₦918 billion, polytechnics more than ₦461 billion, and colleges of education over ₦458 billion [8]. The Fund has executed more than 152,000 infrastructural projects nationwide, including lecture theatres, laboratories, libraries, hostels, and ICT centres [8]. Executive Director Olufemi Lawson noted that institutions previously suffering from severe infrastructural deficits now benefit from significantly improved learning environments [8].

The Ansar-Ud-Deen Society of Nigeria has urged both Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde and President Bola Ahmed Tinubu to expedite the release of 39 students and seven teachers abducted on 15 May 2026 from communities in Oriire local government area [9]. The Muslim organisation's Council Chairman Bashiru Aminu and Secretary Mutiu Alade called the incident unfortunate and worrisome, demanding that governments deploy all necessary resources to ensure the captives return home unharmed [9]. The group emphasised that the rising wave of kidnappings and banditry requires more proactive governmental response [9].

Nigeria's political landscape continues to face significant governance challenges, with civil society groups warning of potential systemic collapse due to accountability failures and weakened democratic institutions [6]. The coalition of scholars and governance experts gathered at the 2nd International Conference on Followership Studies emphasised that governance outcomes depend as much on citizen engagement as on leadership quality, calling for fundamental shifts in democratic culture ahead of the 2027 elections [6]. Recent security incidents have highlighted ongoing challenges, with 39 students and seven teachers abducted from communities in Oriire local government area of Oyo State in May 2026, prompting Muslim organisation Ansar-Ud-Deen Society to urge both state and federal governments to accelerate rescue efforts [9]. The incident reflects broader concerns about rising kidnapping and banditry that continue to affect various regions of the country [9].

Nigeria's higher education sector has received substantial financial support through the Tertiary Education Trust Fund, which disbursed more than ₦1.8 trillion to public tertiary institutions between 2011 and 2024 [8]. Universities received over ₦918 billion, polytechnics more than ₦461 billion, and colleges of education over ₦458 billion during this period [8]. The Centre for Public Accountability's assessment found that TETFund has executed more than 152,000 infrastructural projects nationwide, including lecture theatres, laboratories, libraries, hostels, ICT centres, and faculty buildings [8]. The organisation noted that many public institutions would have faced severe infrastructural decline without these interventions, though challenges remain in procurement processes and project execution timelines [8].

The country's geographic position in West Africa continues to shape its role in regional affairs, with Nigeria occupying approximately 923,769 square kilometres and maintaining 853 kilometres of coastline along the Gulf of Guinea [1][2]. The nation borders Benin to the west, Chad and Cameroon to the east, and Niger to the north, with coordinates placing it between 4° and 14° north latitude and 3° and 15° east longitude [2]. Nigeria's diverse topography ranges from northern savanna regions suitable for cattle herding and grain cultivation to the oil-rich Niger Delta in the south, characterised by mangrove forests and swamps [1].

Taiwan's administrative structure comprises 13 counties, 3 cities, and 6 special municipalities under the jurisdiction of the Executive Yuan [4]. The island nation operates as a democracy, having undergone its first peaceful transfer of power in 2000 when the Kuomintang (KMT) lost to the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP), followed by additional democratic transitions in 2008 and 2016 [6]. Taipei serves as the capital city, located in northern Taiwan [4]. The dominant political issues continue to center on economic reform and growth, alongside the management of relations between Taiwan and China [6]. Taiwan maintains its official designation as the Republic of China, a name retained from when nationalist forces led by General Chiang Kai-shek fled to the island in 1949 following the Communist victory on the mainland [7].

Taiwan's economy has developed into one of East Asia's economic success stories, with a GDP of $611.39 billion and GDP per capita of $32,300 [6]. The island became a major investor in mainland China after 2000 as cross-strait economic ties matured [6]. China serves as Taiwan's biggest trading partner, with many Taiwanese maintaining business connections across the strait [7]. The New Taiwan dollar functions as the official currency [6].

The population of Taiwan stands at 23.60 million people across an area of 35,980 square kilometers, resulting in a population density of 656 people per square kilometer [6]. Polling data indicates an increasing number of residents identify as Taiwanese rather than Chinese over time [7]. The island maintains family and cultural connections with mainland China, though it operates with its own constitution and democratically-elected leadership [7]. Taiwan consistently ranks among the happiest places in East Asia according to United Nations assessments [8].

Taiwan's geographical features include over 100 mountain peaks exceeding 3,000 meters in elevation, with Yu Shan reaching 3,952 meters as the highest point [4][8]. The island experiences significant seismic activity, recording 15,000 to 18,000 earthquakes annually, of which 800 to 1,000 are perceptible to residents [4]. The terrain consists of rugged mountains covering the eastern two-thirds of the island, with flat to rolling plains in the western areas [6]. Taiwan's location places it approximately 160 kilometers off the southeast coast of Asia, bordered by the East China Sea, Philippine Sea, South China Sea, and Taiwan Strait [4].

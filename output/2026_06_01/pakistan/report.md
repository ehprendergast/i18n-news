# Pakistan: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

The Pakistani government has announced its official holiday schedule for the upcoming Eid al-Adha observance in 2026, with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif approving a three-day public holiday period spanning May 26-28, 2026 [6]. The Cabinet Division issued the formal notification for the holiday approval, establishing the framework for nationwide observance [6]. The Central Ruet-e-Hilaal Committee, Pakistan's official moon-sighting body, confirmed the sighting for Dhul Hijjah, formally setting the Eid date for May 27, 2026 [6].

The holiday schedule encompasses all federal ministries, semi-government corporations, and educational institutions across the country [6]. Government offices will remain closed throughout the designated period, with regular operations scheduled to resume on Friday, May 29, 2026 [6]. The announcement follows established protocols for religious holiday observances within Pakistan's federal administrative framework.

Pakistan's banking sector will observe the extended holiday period under State Bank of Pakistan policy directives [6]. Commercial banks will remain closed during the three-day period, aligning with the federal government's holiday schedule [6]. The coordinated closure affects the country's financial infrastructure, with banking operations set to resume alongside government services on May 29, 2026.

Educational institutions throughout Pakistan will observe the holiday period as part of the federal notification [6]. The closure affects schools and universities under federal jurisdiction, creating a unified approach to religious holiday observance across the educational sector [6]. Private sector entities and factories typically follow the same schedule, suggesting broad alignment between public and private sector holiday practices [6].

The timing establishes May 28, 2026 as a public holiday rather than a standard working day, extending the observance period beyond the primary Eid celebration [6]. This scheduling reflects Pakistan's approach to accommodating religious observances within its national calendar, providing extended time for community celebrations and family gatherings during one of Islam's most significant festivals.

# Nigeria: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Nigeria's political landscape remains in flux as parties position themselves for the 2027 elections amid mounting security concerns. The African Democratic Congress has officially announced former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi as running mate to Atiku Abubakar [3], even as an Abuja High Court ordered the Independent National Electoral Commission to deregister the ADC, Accord Party and other political parties [3]. The Peoples Democratic Party secured victories in 20 local government areas during Adamawa council elections [3], while internal divisions persist within the All Progressives Congress, with some party figures criticising primary processes as fraudulent and calling for President Tinubu's intervention [3]. Ogun State selected Kubirat Adegunwa-Balogun as deputy governorship candidate [3], and INEC expanded the candidate list for the Nasarawa North bye-election [3].

Electoral preparations face significant security challenges, with 5,272 deaths reported in five months of 2024, raising concerns about the viability of the 2027 elections [2]. Key electoral threats have been identified as social media volatility, AI-driven disinformation, and foreign information manipulation [2]. Educational security remains problematic, with millions of children studying in buildings lacking proper fencing, lighting, or security personnel [2]. Violent attacks on schools, pupils and staff increased by 40 percent globally, with Nigeria among the affected countries [11]. The Anambra State Government implemented salary cuts for workers over Monday sit-at-home compliance [4].

Nigeria's economic policy framework continues to generate debate between international and domestic perspectives. The International Monetary Fund's policy prescriptions for Nigeria met disagreement from Nigerian economists [2]. The Manufacturers Association reports that policies have yet to translate into measurable industrial growth after three years under the Tinubu administration [2]. President Tinubu appealed to Nigerian youth to remain in the country rather than seeking opportunities abroad, urging them to 'vote, build, work and code' domestically [2].

The digital economy demonstrated significant expansion in 2024, with electronic payment transactions crossing the N1 quadrillion threshold [2]. However, cybercrime continues to drain billions of naira from the economy despite this digital growth [2]. Nigeria became West Africa's largest exporter of petroleum products [4], while naira exchange rate projections suggest potential strengthening to N1,100 per dollar in 2026 [4]. Telecommunications operators face criticism over poor service delivery and rapid data depletion [2].

Nigeria's demographic composition reflects extraordinary diversity, with over 250 ethnic groups speaking more than 500 distinct languages [10]. The Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, and Igbo comprise over 60% of the total population [10]. The country is divided roughly equally between Christians in the south and Muslims in the north [10]. English serves as the official language to facilitate national unity [10]. Educational access varies significantly based on parental financial status, religion, ethnicity, and rural versus urban residence [8].

Traditional family structures remain predominantly male-dominated across ethnic groups [8]. Polygamy is permitted under Islamic law and practiced by some communities [8]. Marriage customs typically involve bridal tokens or dowry payments from grooms to brides' families [8], with women commonly marrying by age twenty [8]. Visiting family and friends constitutes the most popular leisure activity [8].

Football remains Nigeria's dominant sport [8], with wrestling, archery, and horse racing maintaining popularity alongside basketball and polo in northern regions [8]. Nigeria participated in the 2026 FIFA World Cup [2], which provided a platform for social commentary. Manuel Neuer became the seventh oldest player to feature at the World Cup [2]. Musician Davido wore a 'Bring Them Home' outfit at the World Cup to protest insecurity and abductions [7], a gesture that Peter Obi commended [7].

• Educational security deteriorates with inadequate protection for millions of students as global school attacks increase 40%

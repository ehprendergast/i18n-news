**Domestic Politics and Governance**

Nigeria's political landscape remains dominated by positioning for the 2027 elections, with significant party realignments emerging across major political formations. The African Democratic Congress has officially announced former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi as running mate to Atiku Abubakar [3], while simultaneously facing legal challenges as an Abuja High Court has ordered the Independent National Electoral Commission to deregister the ADC along with the Accord Party and other political parties [3]. The All Progressives Congress continues internal reorganisation, with Ogun State selecting Kubirat Adegunwa-Balogun as deputy governorship candidate [3], though some party figures have criticised primary processes as fraudulent, with calls for President Tinubu's intervention [3].

Electoral governance faces mounting security concerns, with reports indicating 5,272 deaths in five months of 2024, raising questions about the viability of the 2027 polls [2]. INEC officials have identified key threats to electoral integrity, including social media volatility, AI-driven disinformation, and foreign information manipulation [2]. At the subnational level, the Peoples Democratic Party secured victories in 20 local government areas during Adamawa council elections [3], while INEC expanded the candidate list for the Nasarawa North bye-election [3].

**Economy, Trade, and Labor**

Nigeria's economic policy framework continues to generate debate between international financial institutions and domestic economists. The International Monetary Fund's latest policy prescriptions for Nigeria have met disagreement from the country's leading economists and financial experts [2]. Manufacturing associations report that despite three years under the Tinubu administration, policies have yet to translate into measurable industrial growth [2]. The naira's exchange rate trajectory remains a focal point, with projections suggesting potential strengthening to N1,100 per dollar in 2026 [4].

The digital economy expanded significantly in 2024, with electronic payment transactions crossing the N1 quadrillion threshold [2]. However, cybercrime continues to drain billions of naira from the economy despite the sector's unprecedented growth rate and accelerating fintech adoption [2]. Nigeria has reportedly become West Africa's largest exporter of petroleum products [4], while telecommunications operators face mounting criticism over poor service delivery and rapid data depletion affecting business operations and financial transactions [2].

**Society, Culture, and the Arts**

Nigeria's demographic composition reflects over 250 ethnic groups speaking more than 500 distinct languages, with the Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, and Igbo comprising over 60% of the total population [10]. The country maintains roughly equal religious divisions between Christians, concentrated in the south, and Muslims, primarily in the north [10]. English serves as the official language to facilitate national unity, though Pidgin and major ethnic languages function as practical lingua francas [10].

Traditional family structures remain predominantly male-dominated across ethnic groups, with polygamy permitted under Islamic law and practiced by some communities [8]. Marriage customs typically involve bridal tokens or dowry payments from grooms to brides' families, with women commonly marrying by age twenty [8]. Educational access varies significantly based on parental financial status, religion, ethnicity, and rural versus urban residence [8].

**Popular Culture, Sports, and Entertainment**

Football remains Nigeria's dominant sport, with wrestling, archery, and horse racing maintaining popularity alongside basketball and polo in northern regions [8]. The 2026 FIFA World Cup has provided a platform for social commentary, with musician Davido using his participation to protest insecurity and abductions through his 'Bring Them Home' outfit, earning commendation from political figure Peter Obi [7]. Visiting family and friends constitutes the most popular leisure activity across the country [8].

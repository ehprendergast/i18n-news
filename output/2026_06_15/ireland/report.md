# Ireland: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Ireland assumed the presidency of the Council of the European Union on 10 June 2026, marking a significant diplomatic milestone for the nation [2]. The country's foreign policy continues to center on its United Nations membership, which has remained a cornerstone of Irish international relations since joining the organization in 1955 [2]. Ireland maintains its position as a central player in shaping European Union economic and social policies, leveraging its role within the continental bloc [2]. Recent diplomatic activity included an official two-day visit by Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, highlighting Ireland's continued emphasis on partnership and heritage connections with diaspora nations [2].

The Irish economy operates with a divided monetary system, with the Republic of Ireland utilizing the euro while Northern Ireland continues to use pound sterling [5]. Transportation infrastructure remains robust, with Dublin Airport serving as the country's largest international gateway and Shannon Airport providing connections to 30 European destinations [5]. Maritime connections facilitate trade through ferry services operating from Dublin, Rosslare, Cork, Belfast, and Larne, linking Ireland to the United Kingdom and continental Europe [5]. The country operates on Greenwich Mean Time plus one hour during daylight savings periods [5].

Ireland's geographical profile encompasses approximately 70,273 square kilometers divided into four main regions [3]. The Central Lowlands contain extensive peat bogs covering roughly 20 percent of the country's landmass, though climate considerations are prompting reconsideration of traditional peat extraction practices [3]. The Western Coastal area includes notable features such as the Cliffs of Moher and Gaeltacht regions where Irish language remains actively spoken [3]. Carrauntoohil stands as the country's highest point at 1,041 meters [3].

Traditional Irish family structures emphasize children residing with parents until marriage, reflecting long-standing cultural patterns [4]. Culinary traditions center on meat, potatoes, and vegetables, with potatoes featuring prominently in various preparations throughout Irish cuisine [4]. The country's diaspora extends to over 70 million people worldwide, creating extensive international networks [2].

Ireland experienced unprecedented weather conditions in May 2026, recording the highest ever May temperatures of 30.9°C in Monastery of Féile, County Limerick and Clonmel, County Tipperary on 25-26 May [18]. These temperatures exceeded the previous May record of 28.4°C set in Ardfert, County Kerry in 1997 [18]. The unusual conditions resulted from high pressure systems from central Europe combining with warmer tropical air masses [18]. May 2026 also marked the driest May since 1941, with maximum daily rainfall reaching only 21.1 millimeters in Roscommon [18].

Social issues continue to present challenges, particularly in Northern Ireland where community tensions persist in Belfast, affecting public transport operations and school schedules [19]. A Commission of Investigation report has criticized failures by senior Garda officers regarding their handling of child abuse allegations dating from 1987 [19].

The country officially recognizes June as Pride month, reflecting institutional acknowledgment of community diversity [2]. Cultural traditions maintain their significance in Irish society, with friendship and family loyalty continuing to hold prominent positions in social structures [4].

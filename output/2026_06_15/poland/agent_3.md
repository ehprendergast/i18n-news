Poland's public debt has reached unprecedented levels, with government borrowing climbing to 2.44 trillion złoty by the end of the first quarter, marking the first time the country has exceeded the symbolically important 60% debt-to-GDP threshold according to EU methodology [8]. The debt now stands at 61.6% of GDP under EU calculations, though it remains within constitutional limits at 50.6% under domestic methodology [8]. The rapid accumulation reflects quarterly increases of 109 billion złoty, outpacing GDP growth of less than 60 billion złoty during the same period [8].

The divergence between EU and domestic debt calculations has widened to 436.1 billion złoty, primarily due to obligations held outside the state budget including 221 billion złoty in the COVID-19 Response Fund and 87 billion złoty in the Armed Forces Support Fund, both housed within Bank Gospodarstwa Krajowego [8]. Annual debt servicing costs have reached 81.7 billion złoty, representing approximately 2.1% of GDP [8]. Current GDP stands at 3.97 trillion złoty [8].

Military procurement arrangements with Ukraine have encountered implementation difficulties, with Poland withholding delivery of MiG-29 fighter aircraft pending completion of agreed drone technology transfers from Kyiv [6]. The transfer arrangement had been structured as a bilateral technology exchange rather than a conventional sale [6].

Infrastructure development continues with authorities announcing the commencement date for construction of the new central airport project, designated Port Polska, though details regarding the future of Warsaw Chopin Airport remain under consideration [4]. The government has unveiled railway investment plans totaling 610 billion złoty, which would substantially reconfigure the national rail network [4].

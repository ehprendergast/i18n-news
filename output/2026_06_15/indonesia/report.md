# Indonesia: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Student demonstrations erupted across multiple Indonesian cities between June 12-15, 2026, marking a significant period of civil unrest [3][4]. The protests, organized by the Alliance of People Calling (Aliansi Rakyat Memanggil), mobilized hundreds of participants in Jakarta, Bandung, Yogyakarta, Semarang, and other urban centers [4]. In Yogyakarta, demonstrators gathered at the strategic Gejayan intersection, while protests in Semarang involved tire burning incidents as students presented five specific demands [3][4]. Following the Jakarta demonstrations, student representatives secured a meeting with Vice President Gibran [4]. The protesters issued an ultimatum to the national legislature, giving the DPR a 72-hour deadline to respond to their demands [3].

Indonesia's fuel pricing landscape underwent substantial adjustments as PT Pertamina announced changes to non-subsidized petroleum products effective June 1, 2026 [6]. Dexlite fuel prices experienced a significant reduction of Rp 3,000 per liter, dropping from Rp 26,000 to Rp 23,000 per liter [6]. The price adjustment applied uniformly across Java, Bali, and eastern Indonesian provinces, including Jakarta, Banten, West Java, Central Java, Yogyakarta, East Java, Bali, West Nusa Tenggara, and East Nusa Tenggara [6]. These domestic pricing changes occurred against a backdrop of declining global oil prices, which followed a peace agreement between the United States and Iran mediated by Pakistan [4]. The international accord is expected to reopen shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz, potentially affecting regional energy markets [4].

Food security conditions in the archipelago remained stable during this period, with regional logistics agencies maintaining adequate supply chains. Bulog Bali confirmed sufficient food stock reserves to last ten months, indicating robust distribution networks in the province [5]. The state logistics agency's assurance provided continuity for essential commodity availability across Balinese markets.

Regional administrative developments reflected ongoing governance activities across the provinces. In Bali, immigration enforcement resulted in the deportation of six foreign nationals for visa violations and property damage [5]. Local emergency response operations concluded search efforts for two children who were swept away by currents at Kuta Beach [5]. Weather forecasting services continued routine operations, with BMKG providing predictions for clear to partly cloudy conditions across Bali's administrative regions [5]. Ferry services maintained regular schedules between Padang Bai and Lembar during the June 15-19 period [5].

Traditional cultural observances maintained their significance within Indonesian Hindu communities throughout the reporting period. Daily calendar consultations continued to guide agricultural and ceremonial activities, with June 15, 2026, identified as favorable for planting betel according to Balinese Hindu traditions [5]. These traditional practices demonstrated the persistence of customary knowledge systems alongside modern administrative structures.

The 2026 FIFA World Cup generated considerable public engagement across Indonesian media platforms, with outlets producing interactive calendars displaying match schedules and results for all tournament fixtures [4]. Indonesian media developed quiz content testing public knowledge of the 48 participating national teams' uniforms, reflecting widespread tournament interest [4]. However, international scientific concerns emerged regarding player safety, as experts warned FIFA that current heat safety measures for the tournament were inadequate and could pose serious risks due to extreme temperatures [4].

Popular culture manifestations of World Cup enthusiasm appeared in various Indonesian cities, with Solo featuring a notable "World Cup Village" displaying 48 national flags throughout the neighborhood [3]. Television programming adapted to accommodate tournament coverage, with Indosiar scheduling special broadcasts that combined football festivities with local talent competitions [3]. These developments illustrated the integration of international sporting events with domestic entertainment formats.

# Bangladesh: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Bangladesh's political landscape has undergone significant transitions following the departure of former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina [7]. The Awami League, which had governed the country since 2008, was removed from power [6][7]. Prior to the Awami League's extended rule, Bangladesh's political system alternated between the Bangladesh Nationalist Party and Awami League from 1991 to 2008 [6]. A new parliament has since cancelled several accountability reforms that were implemented after Hasina's ouster [7].

The economy has demonstrated resilience, maintaining steady growth averaging approximately 6.25 percent annually over the past two decades [6]. This sustained economic performance has contributed to substantial poverty reduction, with rates declining from 11.8 percent in 2010 to 5.0 percent in 2022, based on the international poverty line of $2.15 per day [6]. Bangladesh is scheduled to graduate from the United Nations' Least Developed Countries list in 2026 [6].

Agricultural challenges have emerged across the region, with farmers in Bangladesh and throughout South Asia facing fertiliser shortages and rising costs [7]. The UN Development Programme has warned that fuel and fertiliser supply disruptions could negatively impact crop yields [7]. Bangladesh's agricultural foundation rests on the fertile alluvial plains of the Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta [4], covering approximately 148,460 square kilometers and bordered by India along 4,142 kilometers and Myanmar along 271 kilometers [4][6].

Social structures in Bangladesh reflect traditional values emphasising collective identity over individualism. Extended families often share dwellings due to economic necessity, while nuclear families gain popularity among younger generations [5]. Bangladesh society maintains male-dominated characteristics with diminishing social class roles [5]. Marriage arrangements frequently involve intermediaries called 'ghataks' [5], and men generally marry after completing education or achieving financial stability [5].

Cultural practices emphasise respect and formality in social interactions. Bangladeshis maintain serious facial expressions in public as a sign of maturity [5], and social interaction patterns use respectful suffixes with names and familial terms like 'brother' or 'sister' [5]. Rice serves as the primary staple food, typically accompanied by spicy preparations [5], with food preparation involving shu'ra mixture of chopped onions and spices in warm cooking oil [5]. All vegetables except carrots and cucumbers are fried in food preparation [5], and masoor dal ranks among the most frequently consumed dishes [5].

Bengali language resources continue expanding digitally, serving the world's fourth-largest language by speakers [2]. Sistech Digital developed Bangladesh's first online Bengali dictionary [2], with the platform allowing translation between Bengali and English without software installation [2]. These developments support linguistic accessibility for Bengali speakers globally.

Kabaddi serves as Bangladesh's national sport, featuring teams of twelve players with seven active players competing in two twenty-minute halves [5]. Cricket has provided notable achievements, with Bangladesh's national team securing its maiden home Test victory against Pakistan, aided by Nahid Rana taking five wickets [7]. A former Bangladesh cricket captain assumed leadership of the cricket board after the previous board was dissolved [7].

Health and safety concerns have emerged across multiple sectors. Health ministry data shows 6,476 suspected measles cases among children aged six months to five years [7]. Transportation incidents include a bus accident involving 40 passengers attempting to board a ferry [7]. A large fire outbreak occurred at Kutupalong refugee camp, recognised as the world's largest displacement centre [7].

The refugee situation has deteriorated significantly, with the UN refugee agency warning about South Asia becoming an 'unmarked graveyard for thousands of desperate Rohingya refugees' [7]. Approximately 250 refugees, including children, are missing in maritime incidents according to UN and International Organization for Migration reports [7]. These developments highlight ongoing humanitarian challenges within Bangladesh's borders and regional migration patterns.

Geographic diversity spans from the world's largest river delta to distinct regional areas including the Chittagong Hill Tracts, Sylhet Division, and Barind and Madhupur Tracts [4]. This geographic foundation supports the country's agricultural character while presenting both opportunities and challenges for development across different terrain types.

• New parliament cancelled several accountability reforms implemented after Sheikh Hasina's departure as Prime Minister
• Economy maintained 6.25% average annual growth over two decades with poverty declining from 11.8% to 5.0% between 2010-2022
• Bangladesh scheduled to graduate from UN's Least Developed Countries list in 2026
• Regional agricultural sector faces fertiliser shortages and rising costs affecting farmers across South Asia
• Health ministry reports 6,476 suspected measles cases among young children

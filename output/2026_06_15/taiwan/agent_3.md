Taiwan's geography encompasses 35,808 square kilometers on its main island, with approximately two-thirds consisting of rugged mountain terrain [5]. The Chung-yang Shan central mountain range dominates the landscape, featuring over 200 peaks exceeding 3,000 meters in elevation [5]. Yu Shan, the island's highest point at 3,952 meters, sits within this central mountain region [5]. The island experiences significant seismic activity, recording 15,000 to 18,000 earthquakes annually, with 800 to 1,000 perceptible to residents [5].

Administrative divisions organize Taiwan into 13 counties, three cities, and six special municipalities under Executive Yuan jurisdiction [5]. The counties include Changhua, Chiayi, Hsinchu, Hualien, Kinmen, Lienchiang, Miaoli, Nantou, Penghu, Pingtung, Taitung, Yilan, and Yunlin [5]. Special municipalities comprise Kaohsiung, New Taipei, Taichung, Tainan, Taipei, and Taoyuan cities [5]. Taipei serves as the capital, located in northern Taiwan [5].

Historical surveys indicate shifting identity patterns among Taiwan's population.

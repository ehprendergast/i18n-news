India's manuscript preservation efforts have expanded significantly with the launch of the Gyan Bharatam National Manuscript Survey in March 2026, a Ministry of Culture initiative designed to identify and document manuscripts dispersed across private homes, temples, monasteries, libraries, and institutions [2]. The survey aims to create a geo-tagged national inventory of manuscript repositories and establish a national 'Manuscript Presence Map' for systematic cataloguing and conservation planning [2]. The initiative operates through voluntary participation via a mobile application and website, with authorised surveyors and manuscript custodians able to submit information without affecting ownership or requiring transfer of manuscripts [2]. Traditional cultural practices remain central to Indian society, with the namaste greeting continuing as the standard form of address, varying regionally from "namaskaram" in southern India to "Ram Ram" in rural North India [1].

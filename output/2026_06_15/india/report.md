# India: International Briefing

*Report date: January 20, 2025*

India maintains its position as the world's most populous democracy, with free elections determining leadership since 1947 [1]. The country's administrative structure comprises 28 states and 8 Union Territories [3], governing a territory spanning approximately 3.3 million square kilometres [3]. Recent government initiatives have focused on cultural preservation, with the Gyan Bharatam National Manuscript Survey announced during the Union Budget 2025-26 representing a flagship programme under the Ministry of Culture [2].

The Northern Plains, formed by the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra river systems, continue to serve as the country's primary agricultural hub [3]. Women's workforce participation remains limited, though increasing numbers are joining the workforce in urban areas [1]. India's geography divides into four distinct regions: the Himalayan Region, the Northern Plains river systems, the Peninsular Plateau containing the Malwa and Deccan plateaus, and the Coastal Plains extending along both seaboards [3]. Mount Kanchenjunga represents the nation's highest peak at 8,598 metres, while island territories include Lakshadweep and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands [3].

Traditional cultural practices remain deeply embedded in daily life across the subcontinent. The namaste greeting continues as the standard form of address, with regional variations including "namaskaram" in southern India and "Ram Ram" in rural North India [1]. Family structures emphasise family precedence over individual preferences, with extended families often living together or in proximity [1]. Arranged marriages remain a common practice, while government policies actively address family planning concerns [1].

Dietary patterns reflect both regional and religious distinctions. Rice serves as a staple food in southern India, while wheat bread predominates in northern regions [1]. Religious dietary restrictions continue to influence food customs, with Hindus avoiding beef due to the sacred status of cows, and Muslims abstaining from pork and alcohol [1].

Cultural preservation efforts have expanded significantly with the launch of the Gyan Bharatam National Manuscript Survey in March 2026 by the Ministry of Culture [2]. The survey aims to create a geo-tagged national inventory of manuscript repositories through voluntary participation via a mobile application and website [2]. This initiative addresses the challenge of documenting manuscripts dispersed across private homes, temples, monasteries, libraries, and institutions throughout the country [2].

Social media platforms have emerged as venues for documenting personal achievements and building communities. Anurag Rawat, diagnosed with cerebral palsy at age two, has built an online community of over 200,000 followers through content documenting his athletic pursuits [6]. Rawat completed a 10-kilometre marathon at 12,000 feet altitude and a cycling journey of nearly 300 kilometres, demonstrating the democratising potential of digital platforms [6].

A viral incident at Noida's Supernova Spira Towers involving foreign women smoking by a swimming pool generated substantial online discussion about public behaviour standards [4]. The women allegedly ignored security guard warnings, prompting debates about adherence to housing society regulations and the equal application of community rules [4]. Some reports suggested the foreign women were allegedly smoking marijuana, though this remains unverified [4].

## Key Developments

• The Ministry of Culture launched the Gyan Bharatam National Manuscript Survey in March 2026 to create a comprehensive geo-tagged inventory of India's dispersed manuscript heritage through voluntary digital participation

• Traditional cultural practices including regional greeting variations and religious dietary restrictions continue to define social interactions across India's 28 states and 8 Union Territories

• Women's workforce participation remains concentrated in urban areas despite overall limited representation in the national economy

• Social media platforms have become significant venues for individuals with disabilities to document personal achievements and build large online communities

• Agricultural production continues to centre on the Northern Plains region formed by major river systems, maintaining its role as the country's primary farming hub

• A viral social media incident in Noida sparked public debates about equal enforcement of housing society regulations for all residents and visitors

• Cultural preservation initiatives received significant government budget allocation as flagship programmes, reflecting official prioritisation of heritage documentation

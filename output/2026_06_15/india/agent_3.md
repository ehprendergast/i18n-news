The Indian government launched the Gyan Bharatam National Manuscript Survey in March 2026 as part of a broader cultural preservation initiative announced in the Union Budget 2025-26 [2]. The Ministry of Culture initiative aims to create a comprehensive geo-tagged inventory of manuscripts dispersed across private homes, temples, monasteries, libraries, and institutions throughout the country [2]. The survey operates through voluntary participation via a mobile application and website, with no requirements for manuscript transfer or ownership changes [2]. India's manuscript tradition spans diverse subjects including philosophy, science, medicine, mathematics, astronomy, literature, arts, and governance, though much of this knowledge heritage remains undocumented [2].

India's territorial structure comprises 28 states and 8 Union Territories, spanning approximately 3.3 million square kilometres [3]. The country's geography divides into four distinct regions: the Himalayan Region in the north, the Northern Plains formed by the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra river systems, the Peninsular Plateau containing the Malwa and Deccan plateaus, and the Coastal Plains extending along both western and eastern seaboards [3]. Mount Kanchenjunga represents India's highest peak at 8,598 metres [3]. The nation's island territories include the Lakshadweep coral islands in the Arabian Sea and the volcanic Andaman and Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal [3].

Traditional social structures continue to influence contemporary Indian society, with family units typically taking precedence over individual preferences [1]. Extended families often maintain close geographical proximity, and arranged marriages remain common practice, particularly in non-urban areas, though urban residents increasingly adopt different customs [1]. The namaste greeting, involving pressed palms and specific verbal acknowledgements, remains standard across most regions, with variations such as namaskaram used in southern India [1]. Physical and spiritual purity concepts maintain significant cultural importance, alongside widespread fatalistic perspectives linked to major religious traditions [1].

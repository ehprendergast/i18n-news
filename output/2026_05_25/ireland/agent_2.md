Ireland's geographical composition encompasses four distinct regions across approximately 70,273 square kilometres [3]. The Central Lowlands dominate the interior, featuring flat plains, numerous lakes, and extensive peat bogs that constitute roughly 20 percent of the country's landmass [3]. The Western Coastal area contains rugged landscapes including the Cliffs of Moher, which extend for approximately 14 kilometres along County Clare's coastline, and Carrauntoohil, the country's highest point at 1,041 metres [3]. The region also hosts many Gaeltacht areas where Irish language remains actively spoken [3].

The country's river systems include the Shannon, Ireland's longest river, which flows south from Cuilcagh Mountain through the Central Lowlands to the Atlantic Ocean [3]. Large lakes, known locally as loughs, include Lough Corrib and Lough Ree in the Central Lowlands [3]. The Eastern and Southern Coastal areas feature gentler topography with rolling hills, fertile valleys, and the Wicklow Mountains, which provide a backdrop to Dublin [3].

Finland's domestic political landscape shows continued challenges in youth violence prevention, with police data revealing significant increases in violent crimes committed by minors. Police in Eastern Finland report receiving new videos of underage individuals assaulting their peers nearly weekly, with investigations underway including a recent case in Liperi where multiple minors attacked a 14-year-old [8]. The number of violent crimes committed by those under 15 in North Karelia alone has increased by nearly 200 percent over five years [8]. These incidents increasingly involve premeditated group violence, with perpetrators filming assaults to humiliate victims through social media distribution, often motivated by revenge or status enhancement within peer groups [8].

The energy sector faces mounting concerns about future electricity supply and pricing, particularly regarding industrial competitiveness. Outokumpu, Finland's largest electricity consumer accounting for approximately four percent of national electricity production, has expressed concerns about the long-term electricity market outlook [9]. The company's CEO warned that upcoming data center investments could overshadow planned industrial investments, calling for open, fact-based discussions about Finland's electricity system's 20-year outlook [9]. Government decisions on mining taxes, mine electricity classifications, and the removal of electrification subsidies will significantly increase costs for steel companies like Outokumpu [9].

Infrastructure development efforts address workforce shortages through targeted retraining programs. Destia and Oulu University of Applied Sciences have launched conversion training for construction professionals to transition to infrastructure work, beginning in August with a nine-month paid program combining on-the-job training with 40 credit points of online studies [10]. This initiative responds to Finland's infrastructure sector losing 1,200 workers annually to retirement while educational institutions produce only 600 infrastructure professionals yearly [10]. The construction industry has shed over 30,000 jobs due to poor economic conditions, with residential construction particularly affected, while infrastructure remains relatively stable [10].

Government digital transformation continues expanding with mandatory digital communication for citizens using digital services. Parliament approved legislative changes requiring authorities to send notifications primarily digitally to citizens who use government digital services [23]. The Suomi.fi messaging system has grown by over 800,000 users in two weeks, reaching 3.5 million users total [23]. Citizens authenticating to government digital services from April 14, 2026 onwards will be directed to activate Suomi.fi messaging [23]. Technical issues have affected some users' ability to conduct business on behalf of companies through the mobile application [23].

# Finland: International Briefing

*Report date: 2024-12-19*

Finland's government has implemented significant updates to dual-use goods export controls, with the Ministry for Foreign Affairs conducting training programmes on dual-use product exports [22]. The Ministry of Environment has initiated a review of national land use objectives as part of broader policy adjustments [22]. Parliament has approved legislative changes requiring digital notifications for citizens using digital services, marking a substantial shift towards mandatory digital communication [23]. Citizens authenticating to government digital services from 14 April 2026 onwards will be directed to activate Suomi.fi messaging, with the system having grown by over 800,000 users in two weeks to reach 3.5 million users total [23]. Technical issues have affected users' ability to conduct business on behalf of companies through the mobile application [23].

Finland's infrastructure sector faces acute labour shortages, losing 1,200 workers annually to retirement while producing only 600 new graduates [10]. The construction industry has shed over 30,000 jobs due to poor economic conditions [10]. Destia and Oulu University of Applied Sciences have launched a conversion training programme for construction workers, offering a nine-month paid training programme beginning August 2026 that combines 40 credit points of online study [10].

Energy supply concerns have intensified, particularly regarding industrial competitiveness. Outokumpu, Finland's largest electricity consumer accounting for four percent of national power production, has expressed concern about data centre investments threatening industrial power supply [9]. Chief Executive Kati ter Horst has called for transparent, fact-based discussion about Finland's electricity system outlook [9]. The company seeks partnerships for small modular reactor construction near its Tornio steel plant [12]. Energy storage systems for commercial electric vehicle charging have emerged as critical infrastructure components for grid stability management [21].

Youth violence has escalated sharply across eastern Finland, with crimes by minors under 15 rising 188% in North Karelia and 122% in South Savo over five years [8]. Police receive weekly videos of assault cases involving multiple perpetrators targeting single victims [8]. Attacks frequently involve advance planning and filming for social media distribution, with revenge cited as the primary motive [8]. A recent case in Liperi involved multiple minors attacking a 14-year-old [8].

## Key Developments

• Infrastructure sector launches conversion training to address annual deficit of 600 workers between retirements and new graduates
• Youth violence by minors under 15 surges 188% in North Karelia, characterised by planned group attacks filmed for social media
• Government mandates digital-first communication for citizens using digital services, with Suomi.fi messaging reaching 3.5 million users
• Outokumpu warns data centre investments may threaten industrial electricity supply, seeks nuclear partnership
• Parliament approves legislative framework requiring digital notifications for digitally active citizens starting April 2026
• Government updates dual-use export controls and reviews national land use objectives
• Energy storage systems emerge as critical infrastructure for electric vehicle charging and grid stability

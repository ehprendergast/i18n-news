Ethiopia remains under the leadership of Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, who continues to oversee significant infrastructure developments while managing the aftermath of recent regional conflicts [10]. The federal structure comprises nine ethnically-based regional states including Tigray, Afar, Amhara, Oromia, Somali, Benishangul-Gumuz, Southern Nations Nationalities and Peoples Region, Gambella, and Harari, alongside two administrative councils for Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa [8]. President Sahle-Work Zewde, appointed in October 2018 as the country's first female president, holds the largely ceremonial position while promising to advance gender equality initiatives [10].

The Ethiopian economy is experiencing substantial foreign investment, particularly in agricultural infrastructure. The Dangote Group has expanded its fertilizer plant investment in Gode from $2.5 billion to over $4 billion, with construction progressing on a facility designed to produce 3 million tons of urea annually [9]. This project includes a 110-kilometer pipeline, a 120-megawatt power plant, a polypropylene packaging plant, and an NPK fertilizer blending facility with 2 million ton capacity [9]. Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed recently visited the construction site alongside Aliko Dangote to assess progress, with the shareholders' agreement signed in August and construction officially beginning in October 2025 [9]. The project aims to reduce Ethiopia's dependence on imported fertilizers and support millions of farmers while strengthening domestic production capacity [9].

Ethiopia's population of approximately 110.14 million represents a diverse cultural composition, with the Amhara and Oromo ethnic groups constituting roughly 60 percent of the total population [8]. The country encompasses about 80 nationalities speaking over 80 languages, reflecting Semitic, Cushitic, Omotic and Nilotic peoples [8][11]. Approximately 85 percent of the population resides in rural areas, with an annual population growth rate of about 3.09 percent [8]. The economically active population segment, aged 14 to 60, comprises roughly 50 percent of the total population [8].

Ethiopian culture maintains deep historical roots as Africa's oldest independent country and second-largest by population, having avoided colonization except for a five-year Italian occupation under Mussolini [10]. The nation serves as home to the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, one of the world's oldest Christian denominations, and formerly supported a monarchy that ended in 1974 [10]. Ethiopia operates on a distinctive calendar system that runs seven to eight years behind the Gregorian calendar and contains 13 months [11]. The country's cuisine represents a diverse blend of flavors and traditions reflecting its multicultural population [11].

Football remains the most popular sport among Ethiopians, while long-distance running also enjoys significant popularity [7]. Traditional sports continue to be practiced alongside these modern activities [7]. The media landscape has undergone changes since 2018, though Reporters Without Borders notes that harsh media laws remain largely unchanged [10]. State-controlled broadcasting dominates through national outlets including ETV and Radio Ethiopia, with internet and mobile services subject to restrictions during periods of social unrest [10].

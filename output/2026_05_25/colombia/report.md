# Colombia: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Colombia operates as a republic divided into 32 departments plus a capital district, with Bogotá serving as the national capital [8]. The country's administrative structure reflects its federal approach to governance across a territory covering 1,141,748 square kilometers in northwestern South America [8]. This departmental system provides the framework for political administration across Colombia's diverse geographic regions.

The security environment remains a defining challenge for Colombian governance and institutions. Travel advisories remain in effect for several Colombian departments due to ongoing security concerns [11]. Terrorist groups and illegal armed organizations have expanded their presence into additional regions, complicating state authority and territorial control [11]. These developments affect both institutional capacity and citizen security across multiple departments.

Violent crime presents persistent challenges across Colombian territory, with murder, assault and robbery occurring commonly in many areas [11]. The prevalence of such criminal activity reflects broader security dynamics that intersect with governance challenges. These conditions vary significantly across different departments and regions within the country's administrative structure.

Colombian society exhibits considerable regional variation shaped by the country's diverse geography and climate zones. Cultural practices vary significantly across Colombia's different regions and climates, reflecting the influence of local conditions on social development [10]. This regional diversity characterizes both social organization and cultural expression throughout the country's 32 departments.

Soccer maintains its position as the dominant sport among Colombians, representing a unifying cultural element across the country's varied regions [10]. The sport's popularity transcends regional differences and serves as a common cultural reference point. This reflects broader patterns of cultural engagement that operate alongside the significant regional variations in other cultural practices.

## Key Developments

• Travel advisories remain in effect for several Colombian departments due to persistent security concerns
• Terrorist groups and illegal armed organizations have expanded their operational presence into additional regions
• Violent crime including murder, assault and robbery continues to occur commonly across many areas
• Soccer retains its status as the most popular sport among the Colombian population
• Cultural practices demonstrate significant variation across Colombia's different regions and climate zones
• The country maintains its administrative structure of 32 departments plus a capital district centered in Bogotá

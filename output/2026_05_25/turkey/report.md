# Turkey: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Turkey's approach to the Cyprus dispute remains anchored in its longstanding position favouring a two-state solution. Ankara continues to advocate for the recognition of two equal sovereign peoples and two states on the island, rejecting alternative frameworks for dispute resolution [5]. This position underpins Turkey's broader diplomatic strategy in the Eastern Mediterranean and shapes its bilateral relationships with both Northern Cyprus and other regional actors.

Energy cooperation between Turkey and Northern Cyprus is advancing through concrete infrastructure projects scheduled for 2026. A memorandum of understanding for an undersea natural gas pipeline connecting the two territories will be signed in June 2026, followed by a formal intergovernmental agreement [6]. This development represents a significant expansion of energy ties between Ankara and the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus, with implications for the territory's long-term energy security.

The natural gas pipeline project encounters fewer political obstacles compared to electricity interconnection initiatives in the region [5]. Greek and Greek Cypriot authorities have demonstrated resistance to electricity interconnection projects, citing concerns related to Turkey's status within European Union interconnection frameworks [5]. This differential reception highlights the complex political dynamics surrounding infrastructure development in the divided island.

Northern Cyprus's energy infrastructure will initially utilise the incoming natural gas primarily for electricity generation purposes [5]. This arrangement suggests that the pipeline project serves both immediate practical needs and longer-term strategic objectives for energy diversification in the territory. The project's implementation timeline indicates coordinated planning between Turkish and Northern Cypriot energy authorities.

The infrastructure developments occur within a broader context of regional tensions and competing sovereignty claims. Turkey's energy cooperation with Northern Cyprus proceeds despite ongoing diplomatic disagreements with Greek Cypriot authorities, who Ankara characterises as employing hostile rhetoric toward both Turkey and Northern Cyprus [5].

# Taiwan: International Briefing

*Report date: January 8, 2025*

Taiwan operates under an administrative structure comprising 13 counties, 3 cities, and 6 special municipalities, with Taipei serving as the capital [3]. The 13 counties include Changhua, Chiayi, Hsinchu, Hualien, Kinmen, Lienchiang, Miaoli, Nantou, Penghu, Pingtung, Taitung, Yilan, and Yunlin [3]. This governmental framework oversees a territory that includes the main island plus the outlying archipelagos of Penghu, Kinmen, and Matsu [3][5].

Economic data and trade information remain limited in available verified sources. The territory maintains liberal visa policies, offering visa-free entry to citizens from more than 62 countries for periods ranging from 14 to 90 days [5]. The government operates a 24-hour multilingual travel information hotline to support international visitors [5].

Taiwan's demographic composition reflects centuries of migration patterns. The population of approximately 23.6 million people includes nearly 95 percent who claim Han ancestry, though this represents various Han subgroups with distinct languages and customs [5]. The territory now hosts over 600,000 new immigrants, primarily from China and Southeast Asia [5]. Sixteen indigenous tribes receive official recognition from the government [5].

The island's cultural landscape demonstrates fusion elements from Chinese, Japanese, and indigenous Taiwanese influences [4]. Traditional concepts of "face," roughly equivalent to notions of status and respect, maintain importance in social interactions [4]. Family structures encompass children, parents, extended family, elders, and ancestors, often organized in compact living arrangements [4].

Popular recreational activities among residents include golf, ping-pong, basketball, soccer, softball, and badminton [4]. The mountainous geography influences these leisure patterns, as two-thirds of Taiwan consists of rugged mountain terrain [3]. The Chung-yang Shan range contains over 200 peaks exceeding 3,000 meters in elevation [3].

Taiwan's physical geography significantly shapes daily life. The island covers approximately 35,808 to 36,000 square kilometers and experiences substantial seismic activity, recording 15,000 to 18,000 earthquakes annually, though only 800 to 1,000 are perceptible to residents [3][5]. Yu Shan reaches 3,952 meters and represents the highest point in East Asia [3][5].

## Key Developments

• Taiwan maintains administrative control over 22 territorial divisions including the main island and three offshore archipelagos covering approximately 36,000 square kilometers
• The population of 23.6 million includes 95% Han ancestry from multiple historical migration waves plus 600,000 recent immigrants primarily from China and Southeast Asia
• Sixteen indigenous tribes receive official government recognition alongside diverse immigrant communities
• The territory experiences significant geological activity with 15,000-18,000 annual earthquakes, reflecting its position in a seismically active region
• Liberal visa policies provide visa-free entry to citizens from over 62 countries for periods of 14-90 days
• Cultural practices blend Chinese, Japanese, and indigenous influences while maintaining traditional family structures and social concepts
• Mountainous terrain covering two-thirds of the island influences recreational activities and settlement patterns

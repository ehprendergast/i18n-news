Nigeria occupies a strategic position in West Africa, bordered by the Republic of Benin to the west, Niger to the north, Chad to the northeast, and Cameroon to the east [1][2]. The country extends along 853 kilometers of Atlantic coastline through the Gulf of Guinea, covering approximately 923,769 square kilometers and positioning it as Africa's 14th largest country by landmass [1][2]. Nigeria's geographical coordinates place it between 4° and 14° north latitude and 3° and 15° east longitude [2].

The Niger Delta region serves as the center of Nigeria's oil industry, featuring mangrove forests, swamps, and lagoons where the Niger River system drains into the Atlantic Ocean [1]. Petroleum extraction operations have generated significant environmental challenges including oil spills and land degradation throughout this densely populated coastal area [1].

Nigeria's mineral resources extend beyond petroleum, with the Jos Plateau reaching 1,295 meters in elevation and containing significant deposits that support tin mining operations [1]. The country's highest peak, Chappal Waddi, reaches 2,419 meters in the eastern highlands [1].

The nation's diverse climate zones influence regional agricultural patterns. The northern savanna region features grasslands and scattered vegetation suitable for cattle herding and grain cultivation, transitioning into the semi-arid Sahel zone with sparse rainfall patterns [1]. The Guinea Savanna belt supports cultivation of yam, cassava, and maize [1]. The southern rainforest belt experiences heavy rainfall and high humidity, though extensive deforestation has occurred due to logging and agricultural conversion [1].

## Key Developments

• Nigeria maintains borders with four countries and 853 kilometers of Atlantic coastline, establishing its position as a major West African nation

• The Niger Delta functions as Nigeria's primary oil production center while facing environmental degradation challenges from petroleum extraction

• The Jos Plateau serves as a significant mining region, particularly for tin extraction at elevations reaching 1,295 meters

• Agricultural activities vary by climate zone, with northern areas supporting pastoralism and grains, while the Guinea Savanna belt enables diverse crop cultivation including yam, cassava, and maize

• Extensive deforestation has affected southern rainforest regions due to logging and agricultural conversion

• The semi-arid Sahel zone experiences sparse rainfall patterns, constraining agricultural development in northern areas

# Pakistan: International Briefing

*Report date: December 19, 2024*

Pakistan operates as the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, with Islamabad serving as its capital city [5]. The country encompasses an area of 881,913 square kilometers and shares borders with Afghanistan, Iran, China, and India [5][6]. Limited verified information exists regarding current domestic political developments or governance structures beyond these basic constitutional arrangements.

Pakistan's economy ranks as the 24th largest globally by purchasing power parity [5]. Agriculture employs approximately 39% of the workforce and contributes 19% to GDP, with the country maintaining its position among the world's largest producers of cotton, wheat, sugarcane, and rice [5]. Punjab's fertile Indus River plains serve as the agricultural heartland, supported by the Indus River system that forms the primary water network enabling extensive irrigation across the territory [5][6].

The textile industry represents the largest manufacturing sector and accounts for 60% of national exports [5]. Pakistan possesses significant mineral resources including coal, copper, natural gas, and salt mines, with the Thar Desert containing one of the world's largest coal reserves [5]. The technology sector demonstrates growth patterns, with major cities developing into information technology hubs [5].

Pakistan maintains a population of approximately 241 million people according to the 2023 census, making it the world's fifth-most populous country [5]. Karachi functions as the largest city and primary financial center, housing over 16 million inhabitants, while other major urban centers include Lahore, Faisalabad, and Rawalpindi [5]. The geographic landscape encompasses remarkable diversity, with Gilgit-Baltistan containing five of the world's fourteen peaks exceeding 8,000 meters in height, including K2, the world's second-highest peak [6].

Social customs within Pakistani society emphasize hospitality and treating guests well [4]. The national dress, known as "shalwar-qameez," remains widely worn by men across both urban and rural areas [4]. These traditional practices reflect the continuation of established cultural norms throughout the country.

Cricket and field hockey constitute the most popular sports in Pakistan [4]. Traditional recreational activities persist particularly in rural areas, including hide-and-seek, marbles, kite flying, and gulli-danda, a conventional bat-and-ball game [4]. These activities represent the continuation of established entertainment forms across different regions of the country.

## Key Developments

• Pakistan ranks as the world's fifth-most populous country with approximately 241 million people as of 2023
• The economy stands as the 24th largest globally by purchasing power parity
• Agriculture employs 39% of the workforce and contributes 19% to GDP, with Pakistan among the world's largest producers of cotton, wheat, sugarcane, and rice
• Textile industry dominates manufacturing, accounting for 60% of national exports
• The country contains significant mineral wealth including one of the world's largest coal reserves in the Thar Desert
• Geographic diversity spans from five peaks exceeding 8,000 meters including K2 to extensive river plains
• Technology sector expansion occurs in major urban centers as cities develop into IT hubs
• Traditional cultural practices including national dress and hospitality customs remain prevalent

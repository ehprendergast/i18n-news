Bangladesh's political landscape has undergone significant transformation following the departure of Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina in August 2024 after sustained student-led protests that resulted in hundreds of casualties [3]. The demonstrations initially focused on reforming the quota system that reserved public sector positions for relatives of 1971 independence war veterans, but escalated into broader calls for Hasina's resignation after 15 years of rule [3]. Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus assumed the role of interim prime minister, taking an oath alongside more than a dozen cabinet members at the presidential palace in Dhaka [3]. The military, which maintains considerable influence over Bangladesh's political affairs, applied pressure on Hasina to step down, whilst student leaders explicitly rejected military governance in favour of Yunus's leadership [3]. President Mohammed Shahabuddin, elected unopposed in 2023 as the ruling Awami League's nominee, continues in his largely ceremonial role [3].

Bangladesh's trade deficit reached $24.16 billion in fiscal year 2024-25, according to recent parliamentary statements [4]. The country has sustained nearly six percent annual growth over two decades, with development particularly visible in the capital Dhaka [2]. A 10-member panel has been established to modernise Bangladesh's tea sector [4]. The pharmaceutical industry has contributed one lakh saline units in preparation for dengue season, with the health ministry reporting adequate stock levels [4]. Infrastructure challenges persist, as evidenced by police operating from tents despite a Tk 4,300 crore expressway investment [4]. Labour disputes continue to affect urban areas, with approximately 500 NASSA Group workers blocking the Mohakhali-Tejgaon road in Dhaka over unpaid wages totalling one month's salary [4].

Bengali culture remains central to national identity, with Bangladeshis expressing particular pride in their artistic traditions [2]. The traditionally male-dominated society continues to observe established social customs, including specific dress codes where women typically avoid wearing pants and adults refrain from wearing shorts [2]. Rice serves as the primary food staple, with spicy preparations preferred and dishes often marinated in shu'ra, a mixture of chopped onions and spices [2]. Traditional dining etiquette involves eating with the right hand, which is washed after meals, and food is not passed around the table [2]. Family structures maintain traditional patterns, with few nursing homes and no government retirement programme, leaving grandparents to assist with childcare whilst parents work [2].

Kabaddi holds the distinction as Bangladesh's national sport, involving two competing teams that score points by touching opponents [2]. The Secondary School Certificate examination results are scheduled for publication on July 20, according to the education minister [4]. Social customs around events and gatherings reflect cultural politeness, with individuals often expressing intent to attend functions even when unable to do so, as direct refusal may be perceived negatively [2]. Marriage arrangements frequently involve a "ghatak" or matchmaker, with men typically marrying after completing education or achieving financial security [2].

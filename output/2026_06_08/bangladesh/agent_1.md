Following the resignation of Sheikh Hasina in August 2024 after weeks of student-led protests that resulted in hundreds of deaths, Bangladesh underwent a significant political transition [3]. Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus was sworn in as interim prime minister, taking an oath at the presidential palace in Dhaka alongside more than a dozen cabinet members [3]. The decision to appoint Yunus followed consultations between President Mohammed Shahabuddin, military leaders, and student representatives, with protesters explicitly rejecting military rule in favour of civilian leadership [3]. Mohammed Shahabuddin serves as president after being elected unopposed in 2023, nominated by the then-ruling Awami League [3]. The military, which previously ruled Bangladesh and maintains considerable influence over the country's politics, played a role in pressuring Hasina's departure [3].

Bangladesh operates as a parliamentary democracy with a unicameral legislature, though it remains classified as a developing country with significant infrastructure deficiencies [2]. The nation experienced 15 years of military rule before democracy was restored in 1990, yet the political landscape continues to exhibit volatility [3]. Student protests initially focused on reforming a quota system that reserved public sector positions for relatives of independence war veterans from the 1971 conflict with Pakistan [3]. The interim government under Yunus has emphasised reform as a key priority, with the hope of restoring democratic governance after years of what has been characterised as autocratic rule [3].

**Society and Culture**

Bangladesh maintains a male-dominated social structure, with social classes continuing to play an integral but diminishing role in society [2]. The population takes pride in its artistic traditions, while family structures remain traditional, with few nursing homes and no government-provided retirement programmes [2]. Grandparents typically assist with childcare while parents work [2]. Cultural norms dictate specific dress codes, with women generally not wearing trousers and adults avoiding shorts, whilst rural men commonly wear the lungi [2]. The society operates on principles where direct refusal is often avoided, with people frequently stating they will attempt to attend events even when unable, as saying no may be perceived negatively [2].

**Political Developments**

Bangladesh experienced significant political upheaval in August 2024, when Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina fled to India following weeks of student-led protests that resulted in hundreds of deaths [3]. The demonstrations initially focused on reforming a quota system that reserved public sector positions for relatives of 1971 independence war veterans, but escalated into broader calls for Hasina's resignation [3]. Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus was subsequently sworn in as interim prime minister, taking oath at the presidential palace in Dhaka alongside more than a dozen cabinet members [3]. The transition occurred after consultations between President Mohammed Shahabuddin, military leaders, and student representatives, with students explicitly rejecting military governance in favour of Yunus's leadership [3]. Mohammed Shahabuddin serves as president, having been elected unopposed in 2023 after nomination by the Awami League [3].

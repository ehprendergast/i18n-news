Pakistan: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Pakistan's domestic political landscape has experienced significant turbulence in recent months, particularly in the Pakistan-administered Kashmir region where at least 11 people were killed in clashes between police and protesters [8]. The unrest has prompted authorities to deploy a heavy security presence in Muzaffarabad amid ongoing demonstrations [8]. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Sharif has publicly praised Pakistan's army chief for diplomatic efforts in mediating international negotiations, highlighting the military's continued influence in foreign policy matters [8]. Notably, Pakistan has emerged as a diplomatic mediator between Iran and the United States, securing a tentative agreement after more than 100 days of conflict [8].

The nation's economy maintains its position as the 24th largest globally by purchasing power parity, underpinned by a diverse economic structure spanning agriculture, manufacturing, and natural resources [7]. Agriculture remains a cornerstone of the Pakistani economy, employing approximately 39% of the workforce while contributing 19% to GDP [7]. The country ranks among the world's largest producers of cotton, wheat, sugarcane, and rice, reflecting its agricultural significance on the global stage [7].

Manufacturing is dominated by the textile industry, which represents the largest manufacturing sector and accounts for 60% of Pakistan's total exports [7]. This concentration in textiles demonstrates both the economy's specialisation and its vulnerability to global demand fluctuations in this sector. Pakistan possesses significant mineral wealth, including coal reserves in the Thar Desert that rank among the world's largest, alongside substantial deposits of copper, natural gas, and salt [7].

Pakistan's demographic profile positions it as a major global population centre, with approximately 241 million people according to the 2023 census, making it the world's fifth-most populous country [7]. The nation covers 881,913 square kilometres, with Islamabad serving as the capital and Karachi functioning as the largest city and primary financial centre [7]. Geographically, Pakistan encompasses remarkable diversity, from the Northern Areas of Gilgit-Baltistan containing five of the fourteen peaks exceeding 8,000 metres, including K2, to various other regional landscapes [6].

Pakistani society is characterised by its devout Muslim population, with cultural practices deeply rooted in Islamic traditions and notable differences between ethnic groups, particularly between the more traditional Pashtuns and Baluchis compared to other major groups [5]. Social interactions follow established patterns, with women typically greeting each other through handshakes or embraces, while men are prohibited from touching women in public settings [5]. Family conversations routinely involve discussions about the health and well-being of family members as standard social practice [5].

Marriage customs reflect traditional Islamic values, with arranged marriages constituting the norm and engagement periods varying from months to years [5]. The government's legal framework for marriage recognition operates along religious lines, recognising unions between individuals of the same religious group or between Muslim men and women "of the Book" [5]. Pakistan maintains a notably low divorce rate according to established cultural patterns [5].

Pakistani cuisine centres on chapati and roti as dietary staples, with rice accompanying most meals and tea serving as the predominant beverage throughout the country [5]. These culinary traditions reflect both regional preferences and the agricultural production capabilities of the nation's farming sector.

Cricket remains the predominant sport in Pakistani recreational culture, serving as a unifying element across the country's diverse population [5]. The sport's popularity transcends regional and ethnic boundaries, making it a significant aspect of national identity and popular entertainment.

The intersection of traditional social structures and modern governance continues to shape Pakistani society, with religious principles influencing both personal relationships and legal frameworks. The persistence of arranged marriages alongside low divorce rates indicates the continued strength of traditional family structures within contemporary Pakistani society [5].

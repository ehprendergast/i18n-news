Belgium's aviation sector experienced significant disruption on 2 June 2026, when air traffic controllers at Skeyes conducted coordinated industrial action [10]. The strikes affected flight operations during two distinct periods: morning disruptions between 06:30-09:30 and extended afternoon interruptions from 14:15-21:00 [10]. These work stoppages stemmed from labour disputes concerning working conditions related to the planned implementation of a digital control tower in Namur [10].

The aviation strikes caused substantial operational impacts across Belgium's major airports. Brussels Airport cancelled approximately 140 flights during the strike periods, while some evening departures were potentially rescheduled to operate after 21:00 [10]. Charleroi airport implemented complete flight cancellations for all services scheduled after 14:15 [10]. Liège Airport experienced morning disruptions affecting both passenger and freight operations, with 30 passenger flights cancelled and 40 freight operations impacted [10].

The industrial action reflects broader tensions surrounding Belgium's aviation infrastructure modernisation plans. The disputed digital tower project in Namur is scheduled to centralise air traffic control operations for both Liège and Charleroi airports beginning in 2027 [10]. This technological consolidation has generated worker concerns about operational changes and employment conditions within the air traffic control sector.

China: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Chinese authorities have intensified their supervision of religious activities, detaining two leaders of the Early Rain Covenant Church and taking more than 30 members for interrogation during a Sunday service [5]. The government has also arrested Min Zin, a Myanmar-focused think tank director, on suspicion of espionage while he was in Kunming [5]. These actions reflect ongoing state efforts to maintain control over religious expression and monitor foreign academic engagement within China's borders.

President Xi Jinping completed a two-day visit to Pyongyang, marking his first official trip to North Korea since 2019 [5]. The diplomatic engagement signals continued Chinese investment in bilateral relations with Pyongyang despite international isolation of the North Korean regime. Meanwhile, Hong Kong prosecutors filed the first charges over the deadly Wang Fuk Court fire that killed 168 people, representing Hong Kong's deadliest blaze in seven decades [5].

China's export performance exceeded expectations in May, with growth accelerating to 19.4% year-on-year, surpassing forecasts [3]. The expansion was driven primarily by technology-related shipments, with overall exports of integrated circuits surging 110% in value terms from the previous year [3]. High-tech goods exports jumped 50% in May, demonstrating the economy's continued reliance on technology manufacturing and export-oriented production [3].

Import growth reached 27.4% in May, contributing to a trade surplus of $105.4 billion [3]. The robust import figures suggest sustained domestic demand for foreign goods and raw materials, though the persistent large trade surplus indicates China's export capacity continues to outpace its import consumption across most sectors.

Traditional family structures remain central to Chinese social organisation, with most families saving for years to build their own homes as property ownership represents improved living standards [4]. Parents typically aspire for their children to receive university education and achieve greater prosperity than previous generations [4]. However, traditional family loyalty faces pressure from economic opportunities that are reshaping social dynamics across the country [4].

Regulatory authorities have moved to control 'ghost kitchens'—online food delivery shops that exist only virtually—as part of broader efforts to regulate the digital economy [5]. Police detained a man after videos of dog torture sparked public outrage, reflecting growing public sensitivity to animal welfare issues [5]. These incidents demonstrate the government's responsiveness to social media-driven public concerns.

A notable cultural phenomenon has emerged with 'virtual parents' content creators gaining popularity among young Chinese people seeking parental-style encouragement [5]. This trend suggests evolving family relationships and the role of digital media in providing emotional support traditionally supplied by family networks.

Chinese football referee Ma Ning has attracted viral attention and secured major brand sponsorships for his stern officiating style [5]. Actor Zhang Linghe drew massive crowds at a shopping centre, with fans breaking glass doors in their enthusiasm to see the performer [5]. These events highlight the intensity of celebrity culture and public engagement with entertainment figures in contemporary China.

Taiwan: International Briefing

Report date: January 27, 2025

Taiwan's political leadership continues pursuing expanded international partnerships amid regional constraints. Vice President Hsiao Bi-khim met with a French National Assembly delegation led by Foreign Affairs Committee Secretary Estelle Youssouffa, discussing bilateral cooperation across multiple sectors [8]. The encounter underscores Taiwan's diplomatic challenge of maintaining only 12 official diplomatic relationships plus the Vatican whilst seeking broader international engagement [6]. France's seven-year military planning law identifies freedom of navigation in the Taiwan Strait as a strategic objective, signaling European interest in regional maritime security [8].

The island's governance structure divides administrative responsibilities across 13 counties, 3 cities, and 6 special municipalities under Executive Yuan jurisdiction, with Taipei serving as the capital [5]. This system manages a territory covering 35,808 square kilometers on the main island, where two-thirds consists of rugged mountain terrain [5]. The central Chung-yang Shan range dominates the landscape with over 200 peaks exceeding 3,000 meters, including Yu Shan at 3,952 meters as Taiwan's highest point [5].

Taiwan's economy demonstrated resilience in the first quarter despite global challenges including new tariff measures and rapid artificial intelligence development [8]. China remains Taiwan's largest trading partner, reflecting extensive cross-strait economic interdependence despite political tensions [6]. The government acknowledges adapting to evolving global trade conditions whilst strengthening technological capabilities across multiple sectors.

Seismic activity presents a constant environmental factor, with Taiwan experiencing 15,000 to 18,000 earthquakes annually, of which 800 to 1,000 are perceptible to residents [5]. This geological reality shapes infrastructure planning and emergency preparedness across the mountainous island terrain.

Cultural diplomacy features prominently in Taiwan's international strategy. The territory dispatched a substantial cultural delegation to France during the 2024 Paris Olympics, whilst hosting a French cultural festival in Kaohsiung that attracted significant attendance [8]. These exchanges complement broader cooperation initiatives spanning technology, artificial intelligence, space exploration, and cultural sectors between Taiwan and European partners [8].

Taiwan's society exhibits evolving identity patterns, with polling data indicating increasing numbers of residents identifying as Taiwanese rather than Chinese [6]. This demographic shift occurs alongside maintained economic and family connections across the Taiwan Strait, illustrating the complex nature of cross-strait relationships.

The territory actively strengthens cooperation with democratic partners in Europe and Japan, emphasizing technological collaboration and cultural exchange programs [8]. These partnerships represent Taiwan's approach to expanding international participation despite limited formal diplomatic recognition.

Taiwan-France relations exemplify this diplomatic strategy, encompassing cultural festivals, Olympic participation, and technological cooperation frameworks. The relationship demonstrates how Taiwan pursues substantive international engagement through non-traditional diplomatic channels whilst maintaining focus on democratic partnership networks.

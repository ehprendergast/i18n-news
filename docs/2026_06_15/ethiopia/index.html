Ethiopia: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Ethiopia's electoral commission declared yesterday's general election peaceful, participatory, and inclusive, with monitoring teams covering more than 180 electoral constituencies [1]. The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) reported that no fundamental human rights violations occurred during the election, which met international electoral standards [1]. Of 104 complaints received from various areas, 93 were resolved through information exchange with the election board, while four complaints remain under process and seven require additional information [1].

The country's debt restructuring negotiations continue to face complications as Ethiopia's $1 billion bondholders committee expressed willingness to consider a new government proposal despite rejecting the previous offer last week [1]. However, certain Ethiopian bondholders have indicated intent to pursue legal action [1]. The current negotiations follow earlier setbacks when bilateral creditor countries rejected a January agreement between the government and bondholders for not aligning with Group of 20 framework principles [1].

Religious violence has erupted in the Arsi Zone of Oromia Region, where 13 Orthodox believers were killed in religion-based attacks by armed groups across Aseko, Shirka, and Honqolo Wabe districts over Sunday and Monday [1]. The attacks displaced more than 280 believers from their homes and resulted in the complete destruction of the 101-year-old Tseleta Chefa St. Gabriel Church [1]. Rev. Yilma Terefe confirmed similar attacks in Kara kebele of Aseko district, with residents fleeing to a location called 'Sunte' [1].

Ethiopian Airlines announced the resumption of Kuwait flights from June 9, following suspension due to Middle East conflict [1]. The restoration of this route represents efforts to normalize regional aviation operations after geopolitical disruptions affected connectivity across the region [1].

Cultural programming continues in the capital, with the Addis Ababa University Cultural Center scheduled to present an artistic program featuring singer Tsedenia Gebremarqos [1]. The Desta Jazz Band maintains weekly Wednesday performances at the cultural center located under the Hayat Regency hotel [1].

Key Developments

• Ethiopia's electoral commission declared yesterday's general election peaceful and compliant with international standards, resolving 93 of 104 electoral complaints

• $1 billion bondholders committee rejected Ethiopian government's debt restructuring proposal but remains open to new negotiations

• Religious violence in Arsi Zone killed 13 Orthodox believers and displaced over 280 people in coordinated attacks

• Armed groups completely destroyed 101-year-old St. Gabriel Church in sectarian violence across multiple districts

• Ethiopian Airlines announced resumption of Kuwait flights from June 9 after Middle East conflict suspension

• Bilateral creditor countries previously rejected January debt agreement for not meeting G20 framework principles

• Cultural activities continue with university programs and weekly jazz performances in Addis Ababa

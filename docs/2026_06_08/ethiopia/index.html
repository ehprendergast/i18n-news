Ethiopia: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Ethiopia operates as a federal democratic republic comprising nine ethnically-based regional states and two self-governing administrations [11,15]. Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed leads the government, having assumed office in 2018, while Sahle-Work Zewde serves as the country's first female president [14]. The federal structure encompasses nine regional states and two administrative councils, maintaining a system that reflects the country's ethnic diversity [11]. Ethiopia's governance system represents a continuation of its unique historical trajectory as a nation that has never been colonized except for a five-year Italian occupation during the Mussolini era [14].

Ethiopia's population stands at approximately 110-114 million people, making it Africa's second-most populous nation [14,15]. The country occupies 1,104,300 square kilometers in the Horn of Africa, bordered by Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan, and Sudan [11,15]. Rural areas house approximately 85 percent of citizens, reflecting the predominantly agricultural character of settlement patterns [15].

The nation's linguistic diversity encompasses more than 80 languages, demonstrating the complexity of Ethiopia's cultural landscape [13]. The Oromo and Amhara ethnic groups constitute approximately 60 percent of the population [15]. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church represents one of the world's oldest Christian denominations, highlighting the country's significant role in early Christian history [14].

Ethiopia's topography ranges from Mount Ras Dejen, which reaches 4,533 meters as the highest point, to the Kobar Sink at 125 meters below sea level as the lowest elevation [11]. Addis Ababa serves as the capital within this geographically diverse landscape [11,14].

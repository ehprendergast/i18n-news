Malta: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Malta operates as a parliamentary republic [4] governing approximately 515,000 people [4] across three inhabited islands: Malta, Gozo, and Comino [4]. The nation is subdivided into 68 localities [4], with the capital Valletta serving as the administrative and commercial center. Despite occupying merely 0.61 square kilometers, Valletta holds the distinction of being both the smallest capital city in the European Union [5] and the southernmost capital of Europe. The archipelago covers a total area of 316 square kilometers [4] in the Mediterranean Sea.

Malta recorded the second-largest economic growth in the European Union between the first quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 [2]. This performance positioned the island nation among the strongest economic performers within the bloc during this period. The country's strategic Mediterranean location continues to influence its economic development and international positioning.

Maltese society demonstrates strong family-centered values [3], with extended family relationships remaining central to social organization. The population is characterized by hospitality and generosity [3], maintaining traditional social customs that include arranging visits in advance. Gender roles reflect conventional Mediterranean patterns, with men serving as heads of households while women exercise considerable domestic authority [3]. Children typically maintain close proximity to their relatives throughout their lives.

Social hierarchies manifest through attention to personal presentation, as style and quality of clothing serve as indicators of social status [3]. Maltese people follow European fashion trends [3], paying particular attention to hairstyle and grooming standards. Social gatherings represent a favored activity among the population, reflecting the culture's emphasis on communal interaction.

The nation's cuisine reflects centuries of cultural convergence, demonstrating Italian, English, and North African influences [5]. Malta's culinary traditions combine French, British, Italian, and Arabic flavors [1] developed through historical cross-cultural exchange. The Maltese diet often merges Italian and British foods [3], creating a distinctive Mediterranean fusion that reflects the islands' geographic position and colonial history.

International cultural exchange initiatives have expanded in recent years, with Chinese Guqin concerts taking place in 2026 to strengthen cultural ties between China and Malta [2]. One such concert was held in June 2026 [2], representing broader cultural diplomacy efforts linking the Mediterranean nation with Asian partners. These events complement Malta's traditional European cultural connections.

Football serves as the most popular sport in Malta [3], dominating recreational activities across the islands. The sport's popularity reflects broader Mediterranean sporting preferences and provides a significant avenue for social engagement among residents.

Malta's historical and cultural attractions include substantial UNESCO World Heritage designations, with Valletta alone containing over 300 monuments within its protected status [5]. The Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum, a 5,000-year-old subterranean necropolis rediscovered in 1902 [1], represents one of the nation's most significant archaeological sites. Access to this ancient site is limited to 80 people per day [1] to preserve its fragile interior structures.

Tourism patterns reflect seasonal variations, with July and August representing peak travel months that require advance booking [1] for premium services and accommodations. The concentrated summer season places particular demands on the hospitality infrastructure across the three inhabited islands.

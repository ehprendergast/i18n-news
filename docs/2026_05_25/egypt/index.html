Egypt's fiscal position showed improvement in the 2025/26 financial year, with government revenues rising 34.7% year-on-year to 2.66 trillion Egyptian pounds [13]. The budget deficit narrowed to 5.3% of GDP from 6.2% in the previous year, indicating progress in fiscal consolidation efforts [13]. These developments suggest ongoing efforts to strengthen public finances amid broader economic pressures.

International financial support continues through multilateral channels, with the World Bank approving a $1 billion financing operation to support Egypt's economic reform program [14]. This substantial commitment reflects international confidence in the country's reform trajectory, though specific details of the reform measures remain limited in available documentation.

The Suez Canal Economic Zone has emerged as a significant investment destination, attracting approximately $16 billion of investments over the past three years and nine months [17]. Container volumes at East Port Said demonstrated substantial growth, rising from 2.4 million in 2024 to 5.6 million in 2026, indicating expanding trade infrastructure capacity [17].

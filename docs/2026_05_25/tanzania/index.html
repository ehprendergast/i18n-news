Tanzania: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Tanzania's opposition politics face fresh legal scrutiny as the High Court has ordered Tundu Lissu to respond within seven days to a lawsuit concerning the distribution of CHADEMA party assets [1]. The legal challenge was filed by former CHADEMA Deputy Chairman Said Mohamed alongside two board members, targeting the current arrangements for sharing party resources between mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar [1]. The case involves Ahmed Rashid and the late Maulida Komu as CHADEMA Zanzibar board members, reflecting internal tensions over how the opposition party manages its institutional structure across the union's two territories [1]. The lawsuit specifically challenges the distribution of party assets between mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar, highlighting ongoing questions about resource allocation within Tanzania's federal arrangements [1].

Economic pressures continue mounting across the African continent, with the International Monetary Fund identifying ten African nations facing concerning debt levels by 2026 [1]. High debt burdens owed to the IMF and other external lenders pose significant long-term economic risks for these countries, though specific details about individual nations' positions remain undisclosed in current assessments [1].

Tanzania achieved a significant public health milestone with the official end of its Marburg disease outbreak, declared after 42 days without new infections [3]. The announcement meets World Health Organization criteria for concluding an epidemic, marking successful containment of the viral hemorrhagic fever that had prompted international health concerns [3].

Regional health trends indicate that Type 2 diabetes is increasing more rapidly among certain populations in sub-Saharan Africa than previously estimated [3]. The epidemiological findings suggest shifting health patterns across the region, with implications for healthcare systems and public health planning as dietary patterns and lifestyle factors evolve [3].

Educational disruption affects approximately 17 million children who have been unable to attend school for two years [3]. Girls face heightened risks during these periods of educational interruption, including sexual violence, human trafficking, and forced marriage [3]. The educational crisis extends beyond Tanzania's borders, reflecting broader regional challenges affecting development outcomes across multiple sectors [3].

Broader regional stability concerns include deteriorating conditions in neighbouring countries. The situation in Sudan has reached critical levels, with 16 million children suffering severely due to ongoing civil conflict [3]. The East African Development Community has issued warnings about fighting in South Sudan, stating that recent conflicts are pushing the country towards returning to war [3]. Direct negotiations between the Democratic Republic of Congo and M23 rebels are scheduled to begin in Angola's capital on 18 March [3].

Key Developments

• High Court orders Tundu Lissu to respond within seven days to CHADEMA asset distribution lawsuit filed by former party leadership

• Tanzania officially declares end to Marburg disease outbreak after meeting WHO criteria of 42 days without new infections

• Legal challenge targets distribution of opposition party assets between mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar territories

• IMF identifies ten African nations facing concerning debt levels by 2026, indicating mounting economic pressures

• Type 2 diabetes rates increasing faster than expected among sub-Saharan African populations

• Educational disruption affects 17 million children unable to attend school for two years, with girls facing increased risks of violence and trafficking

• Regional instability persists with 16 million children suffering in Sudan's civil conflict and warnings of renewed war in South Sudan

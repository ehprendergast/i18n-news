Cyprus: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

President Nikos Christodoulides has positioned foreign policy as directly integrated with domestic policy, treating international relations as a tool for economic development [16]. The government has announced plans to expand technical high schools from two to four institutions, implementing curriculum changes that emphasize skills development over memorisation [16]. These educational reforms reflect a strategic alignment of domestic policy with broader economic objectives.

Cyprus recorded 3 percent economic growth in the first quarter of 2026, which represents the highest growth rate in the European Union during that period [16]. Unemployment has fallen below 5 percent, marking a significant improvement in labour market conditions [16]. International rating agencies have provided successive upgrades to Cyprus, reflecting enhanced credibility in the country's economic management [16].

The island nation is experiencing an influx of Indian businesses establishing operations as an entry point to European markets, a development that followed Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit to Cyprus [16]. Indian companies view Cyprus as "a European Hub with an International Footprint," capitalizing on the country's stability and European Union access [16]. President Christodoulides plans to lead a trade mission to Mumbai and New Delhi, focusing on partnerships in technology, innovation, education, energy and tourism [16].

Cyprus maintains its status as the third-largest Mediterranean island, covering 9,251 square kilometres [14]. The island's topography features the Kyrenia mountain range extending across the northern edge and the Troodos Mountains located in the south and west [14]. Mount Olympus serves as the country's highest point at 1,952 metres, while the Mesaoria plain extends across the central portion of the island [14].

Traditional costumes remain part of Cyprus's cultural landscape, worn at folk festivals and special events, including formal visits [13]. This cultural practice demonstrates the preservation of traditional elements within contemporary Cypriot society.

Football commands popularity among both players and spectators throughout Cyprus [13]. The island's geographic features also support winter sports, with three ski runs on Mount Olympus attracting skiers beginning in January [13]. These recreational activities reflect the diverse sporting opportunities available due to Cyprus's Mediterranean climate and mountainous terrain.

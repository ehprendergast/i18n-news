Taiwan: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Taiwan operates as a democratic polity with 22 administrative divisions comprising 13 counties, 3 cities, and 6 special municipalities under Executive Yuan jurisdiction [4]. Taipei serves as the capital city in northern Taiwan [4]. The island underwent significant political transformation beginning in the 1980s, with the Democratic Progressive Party founded in 1986 and martial law lifted in 1987 [6]. Democratic consolidation proceeded through the first legislative elections in over four decades in 1992, followed by the first direct presidential election in 1996 [6]. The political system has demonstrated institutional stability through peaceful transfers of power occurring in 2000, 2008, and 2016 [6].

Taiwan maintains its own constitution and democratically-elected leadership separate from mainland China [7]. The island retains its official designation as the Republic of China, a name carried from when nationalist forces fled to the territory in 1949 [7]. However, only 12 countries plus the Vatican maintain official diplomatic relations with Taiwan [7]. China considers Taiwan a breakaway province and has not excluded the use of force for reunification [7]. Polling indicates increasing identification as Taiwanese rather than Chinese among residents [7].

The economy has achieved developed status with gross domestic product reaching $611.39 billion and per capita GDP of $32,300 [6]. The New Taiwan dollar serves as the official currency [6]. Taiwan became a major investor in mainland China after 2000 as cross-strait economic ties expanded [6]. China now serves as Taiwan's largest trading partner [7]. The island emerged as one of East Asia's economic centres during the latter half of the twentieth century.

Taiwan's population stands at approximately 23.6 million people across 35,980 square kilometres, yielding a population density of 656 people per square kilometre [6]. The island is bordered by the East China Sea, Philippine Sea, South China Sea, and Taiwan Strait [6]. Natural resources include small deposits of coal, natural gas, limestone, and marble, alongside 3,820 square kilometres of irrigated agricultural land [6]. The coastline extends 1,566.3 kilometres [6].

The terrain features mountainous geography covering the eastern two-thirds, with rolling hills and flat plains in the western portion [4][6]. The Chung-yang Shan range dominates central Taiwan with over 200 peaks exceeding 3,000 metres, including Yu Shan at 3,952 metres as the highest point [4]. Over 150 rivers and streams originate in the central mountains, with the Choshui and Kaoping among the most significant [4]. Sun Moon Lake constitutes the largest body of water [4].

Taiwan experiences substantial seismic activity, recording 15,000 to 18,000 earthquakes annually, with 800 to 1,000 perceptible to residents [4]. The island operates nine national parks and features more than 100 hot springs distributed across its territory [8]. Taiwan consistently ranks among the happiest places in East Asia according to United Nations assessments [8].

Cultural practices emphasise the concept of 'face' corresponding to status and respect [5]. Family structures encompass children, parents, extended relatives, elders, and ancestors [5]. Western-style clothing predominates, with business suits considered appropriate for formal occasions [5]. Dining customs centre on food sharing, with communal dish placement for group consumption [5].

Taiwanese cuisine incorporates Chinese, Japanese, and indigenous influences, with xiao long bao (soup dumplings) representing a signature dish [8]. Tea culture maintains significance in social practices [8]. Over 70 night markets operate across Taiwan, with more than 30 located in Taipei, New Taipei, and Keelung [8]. These markets serve as significant social spaces within urban communities.

Popular recreational activities include golf, ping-pong, basketball, soccer, softball, and badminton [5]. The island's entertainment and sporting infrastructure reflects its economic development and urbanisation patterns, though specific data on professional sports leagues or entertainment industry metrics remain limited in available assessments.

Key Developments

• Taiwan operates 22 administrative divisions under democratic governance, having achieved peaceful power transfers in 2000, 2008, and 2016 following political liberalisation in the 1980s

• The economy reached $611.39 billion GDP with $32,300 per capita income, establishing Taiwan as an East Asian economic centre with China as its largest trading partner

• Only 12 countries plus the Vatican maintain official diplomatic relations with Taiwan, while China considers it a breakaway province

• The island experiences 15,000-18,000 earthquakes annually due to active geology, with mountainous terrain covering two-thirds of the 35,980 square kilometre territory

• Population density reaches 656 people per square kilometre among 23.6 million residents, with polling showing increasing identification as Taiwanese rather than Chinese

• Over 70 night markets operate nationwide as significant cultural spaces, reflecting cuisine that blends Chinese, Japanese, and indigenous influences

• Taiwan consistently ranks among East Asia's happiest territories while maintaining separate governance from mainland China under its own constitution

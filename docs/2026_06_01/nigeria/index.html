Nigeria: International Briefing

Report date: December 19, 2024

Nigeria's political landscape faces mounting pressure as civil society groups warn of potential systemic collapse due to accountability failures and weakened democratic institutions ahead of the 2027 elections [6]. The 2nd International Conference on Followership Studies, organised by PANAFSTRAG in collaboration with Empowered Newswire and anchored by veteran journalist Laolu Akande, brought together scholars and governance experts who emphasised that governance outcomes depend as much on citizen engagement as on leadership quality [6]. The event was dedicated to the memory of Mallam Aminu Kano, described as a defining symbol of citizen-centred political engagement in Nigeria's democratic evolution [6].

Security challenges continue to plague various regions, with 39 students and seven teachers abducted from communities in Oriire local government area of Oyo State on May 15, 2026 [9]. The Ansar-Ud-Deen Society urged both Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde and President Bola Ahmed Tinubu to expedite the release of the abducted victims, highlighting concerns about the rising wave of kidnapping and banditry affecting the country [9].

Nigeria's higher education sector has received substantial financial support through the Tertiary Education Trust Fund, which disbursed more than ₦1.8 trillion to public tertiary institutions between 2011 and 2024 [8]. Universities received the largest allocation of over ₦918 billion, while polytechnics received more than ₦461 billion and colleges of education received over ₦458 billion during this period [8]. TETFund has executed more than 152,000 infrastructural projects nationwide, including lecture theatres, laboratories, libraries, hostels, and ICT centres [8].

The country's geographic position continues to shape its regional role, occupying approximately 923,769 square kilometres in West Africa [1]. Nigeria maintains 853 kilometres of coastline along the Gulf of Guinea and borders Benin to the west, Chad and Cameroon to the east, and Niger to the north [1].

Nigeria's swimming community has shown significant development through the Dolphin Swimming League, which completed its seventh season with notable achievements. Naetochukwu Oguegbu of Meadow Hall School won the Best Male Swimmer award with 15 gold medals, while Oluwatofunmi Ibidapo of Grange School retained the Best Female Swimmer title for the second consecutive year with 14 gold medals and one silver [7]. Former Nigeria Aquatics Federation President Babatunde Fatayi-Williams described the Dolphin Swimming League as the best competition to emerge from Nigeria [7].

In team competitions, St Saviour's School, Ikoyi, accumulated 954.5 points to win the Best Primary School title, while Grange School topped the secondary school category with 1,074 points [7]. Grange School and St Saviour's School retained their titles as Nigeria's top-ranked swimming schools [7].
